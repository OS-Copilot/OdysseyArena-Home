OdysseyArena

Benchmarking Large Language Models for Long-Horizon, Active and Inductive Interactions

Most agent benchmarks hand the agent the rules and score how well it follows them. OdysseyArena hides them. Its environments evolve under latent transition laws that an agent must discover through its own actions, over hundreds of steps, before it can plan.

  • Inductive evaluation: four structural primitives, from Boolean couplings to dependency graphs, each turned into an environment whose rules are never shown to the agent
  • OdysseyArena-Lite and OdysseyArena-Challenge: a standardized suite for routine evaluation, and a stress test whose trajectories run past a thousand steps
  • Findings on frontier LLMs: near-perfect once the rules are given, far below humans without them, with the failures traced to exploration, memory and action loops rather than planning

Overview

Agents that must discover the rules

Interactive benchmarks so far test a deductive mode: the agent is told how the world works and is scored on carrying out a task in it. Deployment also needs the inductive mode, where the agent works out how the world works from what its actions do.

Deductive

The transition rules arrive in the prompt. Success measures how well the agent applies them.

Inductive

Nothing about the dynamics is given. The agent has to probe, read the feedback and build the rules itself.

Extremely long horizons

Episodes of hundreds to thousands of steps, where strategic coherence and accumulated error decide the outcome, where most suites stop under fifty.

Active exploration

No fully specified success criterion to execute against. The agent probes, reacts to feedback and revises its own strategy as it goes.

Inductive reasoning from interaction

Latent transition laws are inferred from real-time experience, which is what lets an agent anticipate outcomes and plan instead of react.

  1. A new perspective for agent evaluation. Agents are scored on what they can discover about an environment's dynamics through long-horizon, active interaction, rather than on following rules they are handed.
  2. Reliable and scalable evaluation. Four environments built on four structural primitives, each with an unbounded task space; OdysseyArena-Lite fixes a standard suite for efficient, reproducible comparison and OdysseyArena-Challenge stress-tests stability over extreme horizons.
  3. Extensive evaluation and insights. Frontier and open-weight LLMs, with an analysis that locates the bottleneck in inductive discovery: exploration, memory and action loops, not planning.
Environments

Four primitives, four environments

Every environment is a transition function the agent cannot see. Each one isolates a different kind of structure to be discovered: Boolean couplings, factors under noise, periodic cycles, and a dependency graph. All four run as lightweight APIs, with no simulator or container to deploy.

Trajectories follow the demonstrations in the paper; the prices, news and package names are schematic. Use the arrow keys or Play to step through.

Benchmark

Two suites from one task space

Each environment generates tasks from a bounded parameter range. A task is a structural configuration that stays fixed for the episode plus a pre-computed temporal trajectory, so every model faces exactly the same world and every instance is verified solvable.

From a configuration to a verdict

Results

How far frontier models get

Proprietary and open-weight LLMs on OdysseyArena-Lite, four runs per task, against human participants who solved the same instances by trial and error.

A
A wide gap to humans

Human participants outperform every evaluated model on Avg@4 in Turn On Lights, AI Trading and Repo System. Gemini 3 Pro Preview leads on Energy Dispatch.

B
Deductive proficiency, inductive deficiency

Given the latent rules in the prompt, frontier models reach near-perfect success on Turn On Lights. Without them the same models fall to a fraction. The bottleneck is discovering the dynamics, not executing them.

C
Periodic patterns defeat almost everyone

Energy Dispatch needs about twenty steps of observation to pin down an efficiency cycle. Nearly every model fails every task there, and scale alone does not change that.

Analysis

Where the runs go wrong

Failed trajectories were read by hand and the behaviour measured over every run: what a model does with more steps, how often it repeats a rejected action, and which interventions move the number.

More steps stop helping

Cumulative share of tasks solved by each step, or profit rate for AI Trading. Most success curves flatten after an early exploratory phase; a longer budget does not substitute for a model of the dynamics. The weakest models track or trail a random policy.

Action loops

The share of a model's steps spent immediately repeating an action the environment just rejected, against its success rate. Looping is inductive stagnation made visible: the model does not turn the failed trial into a revised rule.

What went wrong in the failed runs

Every failed trajectory of Gemini 3 Pro Preview, read and labelled by hand; one trajectory can carry several modes. Planning is almost never the problem.

More reasoning helps

gpt-oss-120b at three reasoning budgets. Higher effort improves Turn On Lights and AI Trading. Repo System dips at medium effort; Energy Dispatch stays at zero.

A longer memory does not

Repo System with more or fewer turns of history in the prompt. A window of about a hundred turns does best; the full history does worse.

A worked example does not either

One complete solution in the prompt leaves Repo System success where it was. Eliciting induction takes more than a demonstration.
